portly

Privacy policy

Effective October 29, 2026

Portly is a self-hosted browser home page. It runs either as a small server on your own Windows PC, listening only on that computer (localhost; your other PCs can reach it only through a reverse proxy you run yourself, such as Tailscale, and only after you approve each of them on that PC), or as a browser-only site served from GitHub Pages that keeps its data in the browser. There is no Portly cloud service: its developer, davideAurum, does not operate any server that receives, stores or processes your data. Portly was called Homebase until October 2026; this policy replaces the one published under that name.

Data Portly accesses from Google

When you connect a Google account and approve access on Google's consent screen, Portly can access:

  • Account identity (openid, userinfo.email, userinfo.profile): your email address and name, used to label the connected account in Portly.
  • Calendar list (calendar.calendarlist.readonly): the names and colors of your calendars, so you can choose which ones to show.
  • Calendar events (calendar.events): event titles, times, locations and descriptions, to display your calendar and to create, edit and delete events when you ask it to.
  • Gmail (gmail.modify): the unread count and, for recent inbox messages, the sender, subject, date and Google's short preview snippet. Portly does not download message bodies or attachments. It uses this permission only to mark messages read or unread, archive them, or move them to the trash when you ask it to. It never sends mail and never permanently deletes mail.

Portly asks only for the services you tick when you connect the account. Google accounts can be connected to Portly on your PC only, not to the browser-only site.

Data Portly accesses from Microsoft (Outlook.com)

When you connect a personal Microsoft account and approve access on Microsoft's consent screen (the sign-in at login.microsoftonline.com; your data then goes through Microsoft Graph, graph.microsoft.com), Portly can access your account identity (openid, profile, email, User.Read) to label the account, can stay signed in without asking you again (offline_access), and, for the services you tick:

  • Calendar (Calendars.ReadWrite): your calendars and their events, to display them and to create, edit and delete events when you ask it to.
  • Mail (Mail.ReadWrite): the unread count and, for recent inbox messages, the sender, subject, date and preview. Portly uses this permission only to mark messages read or unread, archive them, or move them to the trash when you ask it to. It never sends mail and never permanently deletes mail. Mail is available only in Portly on your PC; the browser-only site never asks for it.
  • Launch pad sync (Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder), on the browser-only site only, when you tick it: a folder of Portly's own in your OneDrive (under Apps, named after your app registration), and nothing else in your OneDrive. Portly keeps your launch pad there, as described under Launch pad sync.

Other accounts and addresses you add

  • Yahoo (caldav.calendar.yahoo.com, imap.mail.yahoo.com) and other CalDAV or IMAP servers, with an app password you create at the provider: calendars (read-only) and, for mail, the same inbox summary and actions as described for Microsoft above.
  • Todoist (api.todoist.com), with a personal API token: your tasks and projects, and the tasks you add, edit, complete or reopen.
  • GitHub (api.github.com), on the browser-only site, for launch pad sync: a private repository you create and a fine-grained personal access token you limit to it. Portly uses them only for that repository: it checks that the repository is private, and reads, writes and removes its own files there (see Launch pad sync).
  • Calendar subscriptions (ICS or webcal links) and RSS or Atom feeds: Portly on your PC fetches the addresses you add; the browser-only site does not offer them.

How the data is stored

Portly on your PC:

  • OAuth tokens, app passwords, API tokens, the OAuth client secret you enter for Google and the addresses of calendar subscriptions are stored only on your PC, encrypted (AES-256-GCM under a key protected by Windows DPAPI), and are never returned to the browser.
  • Calendar events are cached in a local database on your PC so the page loads quickly.
  • Mail is never written to disk; message lists are held in memory for about a minute.
  • Bookmark icons that were fetched are kept on your PC.
  • So that the page can appear at once, your browser also keeps a snapshot of what it last showed, including the calendar events on screen but never mail, in its local storage for Portly's address. The snapshot is dropped after 24 hours or when Portly is updated.
  • If you let your other PCs use Portly on this PC (through a reverse proxy you run, after approving each PC here), Portly keeps on this PC, for each PC that asks to connect, the name you gave it (filled in from that PC's computer name when Tailscale on this PC can tell who is asking; that name is held in memory for a few minutes and never written down, and nothing is stored until you ask to connect), a short description of its browser and system (such as "Chrome on Windows"), when it asked, connected and last used Portly, and a hash of its login cookie, never the cookie itself; a removed PC's record is kept for 30 days so a stale cookie is recognised. Each approved PC's browser keeps the login cookie for Portly's address, which lasts 30 days of inactivity. Removing a PC in Settings ends its access at once. Portly still listens only on this PC; the proxy is yours, and no Portly service is involved.

The browser-only site:

  • Settings, layouts, bookmarks, uploaded images, the Todoist token, the tokens of your Microsoft sign-in, the GitHub repository name and token for launch pad sync and a copy of your Outlook.com calendars are stored by the browser, in its storage for the site's address (IndexedDB), on that device. Portly does not encrypt them; they are as safe as the browser profile they live in.
  • The browser keeps the same kind of snapshot for the site, including the calendar events on screen, dropped after 24 hours or when Portly is updated.
  • None of it is sent to Portly's developer, and none to GitHub, which serves the page, except what launch pad sync writes to your own repository if you choose GitHub for it. Data moves to another browser or device only when you export it and import it there, or, for the launch pad, through launch pad sync.

If you pair the browser-only site with Portly on your PC (hybrid mode, described below), the site shows and changes the data on your PC instead, under the rules for Portly on your PC above, and the browser keeps a snapshot of what the site last showed (never accounts or mail) for up to 7 days, next to the pairing token, in its local storage for the site.

Launch pad sync

The browser-only site can keep its launch pad the same in several browsers. It is off until you set it up, and each browser syncs through one connection:

  • OneDrive, through an Outlook.com connection with Launch pad sync ticked: the browser talks directly to Microsoft Graph (graph.microsoft.com) with the account's access token, and downloads the files from OneDrive's download host (my.microsoftpersonalcontent.com) without it. The files are kept in your OneDrive under Apps, in the folder named after your app registration.
  • GitHub, through a private repository you create: the browser talks directly to api.github.com, with your token and the repository name in every request. The files are kept in that repository. Every change is a commit, so its history keeps earlier versions, removed bookmarks included, until you delete the repository.

The synced files hold your bookmark groups (names, colors, order), your bookmarks (titles, addresses, their group and order, icon choice), the custom icon images you picked, which group is the default, and, for 90 days, a record of each group or bookmark you removed, with its last content, so that other browsers remove it too. Layouts, the theme, other settings, accounts, tokens and calendars are never synced. The browser checks the files when the page loads, when it gets focus and every 5 minutes while it is visible, and writes them after changes. Nothing goes to a Portly service: Microsoft or GitHub keeps the files, like your other files there, and Portly does not encrypt them.

Sharing

Portly does not sell, share or transfer Google user data, or any other data from the accounts you connect, to anyone. The only network traffic carrying data from those accounts is between your PC or browser and that provider's own services. Portly has no analytics, advertising or tracking, and does not use Google user data to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.

Limited Use

Portly's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Services that receive data from Portly

Besides the accounts you connect, these services receive data when the feature that uses them is on:

  • Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com, geocoding-api.open-meteo.com): the coordinates of the location you choose for the weather, and the text you type when searching for a place.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org): when the weather widget uses this device's location, those coordinates (rounded to about 1 km), to show the name of the town you are in. Each spot is looked up at most once a week.
  • ESPN (site.api.espn.com, team logos from a.espncdn.com): which leagues and teams you follow in the sports scores widget.
  • Search suggestions: while suggestions are on, what you have typed so far goes to the suggestion provider of the search engine you are using: Google (suggestqueries.google.com, also for YouTube), DuckDuckGo (duckduckgo.com), Bing (api.bing.com) or Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org). The browser-only site can ask only Wikipedia: when you search with the Wikipedia engine, or in place of the other engines' suggestions only if you turn on Wikipedia suggestions in Settings → Search.
  • Bookmark icons and titles: when you add or edit a bookmark, Portly on your PC fetches the page at the address you typed to suggest its title and icon. For a bookmark set to an automatic icon, and for the sites of your search engines while automatic icons are on, it asks the website itself first, then the DuckDuckGo icon service (icons.duckduckgo.com) and Google's favicon service (www.google.com/s2/favicons), which receive the site's host name, not the full address. The browser-only site uses only the DuckDuckGo icon service. Hosts that look private are never looked up, and you can choose a custom icon or a letter tile instead.
  • Calendar subscriptions and feeds: the servers at the addresses you add see the request, like any feed reader.
  • Links you click: opening a message or task from Portly takes you to the provider's own site (Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo Mail, Todoist), with that message or task named in the address, like any link.
  • GitHub Pages, when you use the browser-only site, or when you have told Portly on your PC about your site (Portly's own page then checks that the site can be reached and opens it there): your IP address, like for any website.
  • Portly on this PC (http://127.0.0.1:5757, hybrid mode): when Portly also runs on the PC you browse from, the browser-only site checks on each page load whether it is running (your browser may first ask you to allow the site to reach this PC) and, after you have paired that browser on Portly's own page, sends every request of the page to it with the pairing token and shows Portly's data instead of the browser's copy. Nothing leaves the PC.
  • GitHub (github.com), when you install or update Portly on your PC with its install script: the download of the release, like any software download.

The new-tab extension

The Portly extension for Chrome and Edge replaces the browser's New Tab page with Portly. It talks only to Portly on your PC (localhost) and to Portly's launcher on your PC, which starts the server when needed; if you have told Portly about your browser-only site, a new tab opens that site. Its toolbar button reads the address and title of the current tab only when you click it, to add that page as a bookmark. When Portly cannot be started, the page it shows instead has a plain Google search box; what you type there goes to Google (www.google.com) only when you submit it, like any search. The extension keeps only its own settings and what Portly last told it about its browser-only site in the browser, makes no request to any other website and collects nothing.

Removing access and deleting data

  • Removing an account in Portly (Settings → Accounts) deletes its tokens and cached data from your PC or browser and, for Google, revokes Portly's access at Google.
  • You can also revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections or, for Microsoft, at account.live.com/consent/Manage.
  • Turning launch pad sync off, or removing its connection, stops it in that browser and leaves the synced files where they are, in your OneDrive (under Apps) or your GitHub repository, until you delete them there. Removing GitHub in Settings → Accounts deletes the repository name and token from the browser; revoke the token in your GitHub settings.
  • Uninstalling Portly and deleting its data folder removes everything it stored on your PC; clearing the browser's data for Portly's address removes the browser's snapshot. Clearing the browser's data for the site removes everything the browser-only site stored, the pairing included.
